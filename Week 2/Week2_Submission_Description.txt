Week 2 focused on creating a basic financial forecast using the same banking-system indicator framework established in Week 1. I selected India’s bank non-performing loans (NPL) to total gross loans ratio because it is a relevant measure of asset quality and credit risk, and because a historical time series is available for forecasting. The forecast extends the Week 1 analysis from a cross-country snapshot into a time-series exercise.

The report uses annual observations for India from 2011 through 2023. The historical series is based on the World Bank World Development Indicators indicator FB.AST.NPER.ZS. The World Bank defines this indicator as the proportion of a deposit taker’s loan portfolio that is impaired or at risk of default. The model uses a three-year simple moving average, selected as a transparent baseline appropriate for a beginner-level forecasting task and a relatively small annual dataset. The model smooths short-term fluctuations and avoids imposing a strong parametric trend on a banking-risk series that can be cyclical.

The model is implemented recursively to produce forecasts for 2024, 2025, and 2026. A rolling one-step backtest is also included to assess historical forecast error, using mean absolute error and root mean squared error. The forecast results should be interpreted as a simple analytical baseline rather than a definitive prediction of future banking conditions. In particular, NPL ratios can be affected by economic growth, credit cycles, regulation, provisioning practices, and financial shocks that are not explicitly included in this model.

The completed report documents the indicator selection, data source, model design, implementation, forecast results, validation approach, interpretation, and limitations. The supporting CSV, Python script, and forecast visualization are included as reproducible project files.
