This Week 3 report presents a structured risk analysis of the banking-system indicators used throughout the financial-data project. The analysis builds directly on the Week 1 World Bank dataset and the Week 2 India non-performing loan forecast, maintaining continuity across the internship tasks. The report identifies six major analytical and financial risks: asset-quality deterioration, forecast/model uncertainty, data comparability and timing risk, credit-cycle and concentration risk, capital adequacy pressure, and over-reliance on aggregate indicators.

Each risk is assessed using a simple likelihood-impact framework. Likelihood and impact are classified as Low, Medium, or High, and a qualitative priority score is calculated by multiplying the two levels. This produces a transparent risk register that can be reviewed and updated as additional information becomes available. The report also presents a risk matrix to visually communicate which risks deserve greater attention.

The analysis highlights the importance of non-performing loans as an asset-quality indicator. India's historical NPL series demonstrates that asset quality can change substantially over time, while the Week 2 forecast illustrates why model uncertainty should be considered when using projected values. The report also emphasizes that cross-country comparisons require careful attention to observation years because the latest available data do not necessarily refer to the same period.

Mitigation strategies are proposed for each identified risk. These include monitoring early-warning indicators, maintaining capital buffers, conducting stress tests, improving data alignment, evaluating credit concentration, validating forecasting models, and expanding the analytical dashboard with additional macro-financial indicators. The report concludes with limitations and recommendations for future analysis, emphasizing that the exercise is a structured risk-screening framework rather than a regulatory stress test or investment recommendation.
