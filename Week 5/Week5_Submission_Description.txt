This Week 5 report focuses on data visualization using the banking and financial indicators developed during the previous weeks. The objective is to communicate financial patterns and comparisons clearly rather than simply producing charts. The analysis uses three indicators: bank capital to assets, non-performing loans as a percentage of gross loans, and private-sector credit provided by banks as a percentage of GDP. These indicators represent complementary dimensions of banking-system capitalization, asset quality and credit depth.

Six visual outputs are included. Three ranked horizontal bar charts compare countries on bank capitalization, NPL ratios and private-sector credit. Horizontal bars were selected because they make country names readable and allow the reader to identify relative rankings quickly. Two scatter plots examine relationships between capitalization and NPLs, and between private credit and NPLs. Scatter plots are appropriate because both variables are continuous and the objective is to explore potential relationships and identify outliers. A correlation matrix provides a compact numerical summary of the pairwise linear associations among the indicators.

The report also explains the design principles behind each visualization, including sorting, labeling, unit selection and appropriate use of chart types. The findings are presented carefully because the Week 1 dataset contains 15 countries and the latest available observation year can differ across indicators and countries. Therefore, the visualizations are exploratory rather than synchronized time-series comparisons. Correlations are also not interpreted as evidence of causation.

Overall, the exercise demonstrates how effective financial visualization should connect chart choice to the analytical question. The resulting charts make the underlying dataset easier to compare, interpret and communicate, while the discussion of limitations prevents overinterpretation of aggregate cross-country relationships.
