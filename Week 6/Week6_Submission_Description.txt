This final Week 6 report consolidates the financial-data analysis completed across the internship project. The work began with a 15-country World Bank dataset covering bank capital to assets, non-performing loans as a percentage of gross loans, and private-sector credit as a percentage of GDP. Week 1 established the data-acquisition, cleaning, exploratory-analysis and visualization foundation. Week 2 extended the project into forecasting by applying a transparent three-year moving-average baseline to India's historical NPL series and producing forecasts for 2024–2026. Week 3 converted the financial indicators into a structured risk-analysis framework covering asset quality, capital pressure, credit-cycle risk, forecast uncertainty and data limitations. Week 4 applied formal hypothesis testing to the India NPL time series. Week 5 focused on financial storytelling through ranked bar charts, scatter plots and a correlation matrix.

The final report summarizes the methods, results and lessons from each stage and then integrates them into broader financial insights. A key conclusion is that banking-system condition cannot be assessed using a single metric. Capitalization, asset quality and credit depth capture different dimensions and should be evaluated together. The project also demonstrates that statistical significance and forecasting output must be interpreted with caution when datasets are small, observations are not synchronized and important economic variables are omitted.

As part of final quality control, a consistency issue was identified between the historical India NPL values stored for Week 2 and those stored for Week 4. The final report explicitly documents this issue and uses the Week 2 historical series—the series underlying the forecast—as the consistent basis for the final time-series summary and recomputed hypothesis test. This is important because a professional analytical workflow should not silently combine different versions of the same dataset.

The report concludes with recommendations for future analysis, including synchronized cross-country panels, longer time series, alternative forecasting methods, macroeconomic explanatory variables, bank-level indicators, stress testing and interactive dashboards. Overall, the project demonstrates a complete financial-data workflow from public data acquisition through analysis, statistical testing, risk assessment, visualization and final communication.
