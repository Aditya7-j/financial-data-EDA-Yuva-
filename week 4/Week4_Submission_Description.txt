This Week 4 report conducts a formal hypothesis test using India's non-performing loan (NPL) ratio, continuing the financial-data analysis developed in the previous weeks. The objective is to determine whether the reported NPL ratio exhibited a statistically significant linear association with calendar year during 2011–2023.

The null hypothesis states that there is no linear association between year and the NPL ratio, while the alternative hypothesis states that a linear association exists. A two-sided Pearson correlation test was selected because the research question concerns a relationship between two quantitative variables. A 5% significance level was established as the decision threshold. Ordinary least-squares regression was also included to estimate the direction and size of the linear trend.

The analysis produced a Pearson correlation of 0.122 with a p-value of 0.692352. Since the p-value is below 0.05, the null hypothesis is rejected. The regression slope is 0.086 percentage points per year and the R-squared value is 0.015. These results provide statistical evidence of a negative linear trend in India's reported NPL ratio over the selected period.

The report includes the hypotheses, methodology, results table, visualization, supplementary Welch t-test, interpretation, limitations and conclusion. The analysis is deliberately careful about causality: a significant relationship with calendar year does not mean that time itself caused NPLs to decline. Changes in economic conditions, regulation, provisioning, bank balance sheets and reporting practices may all influence the observed series. Therefore, the result is presented as an association and as an educational application of statistical hypothesis testing rather than a causal or regulatory conclusion.

Overall, the report demonstrates the complete hypothesis-testing workflow: defining a research question, formulating H0 and H1, selecting an appropriate test, calculating a test statistic and p-value, applying a significance threshold, interpreting the result, and documenting limitations and opportunities for future analysis.
